Stroke: know the signs and act FAST

A stroke is a medical emergency. Knowing the signs, and planning where to go before you need to, can save a life.

By Ilé Editorial Team20 October 20262 min read

A stroke happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is blocked, or when a blood vessel in the brain bursts. Brain cells start to die within minutes. The faster someone gets to hospital, the better their chance of survival and recovery.

Remember FAST

  • Face: ask them to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
  • Arms: ask them to raise both arms. Does one arm drift down or feel weak?
  • Speech: ask them to repeat a simple sentence. Is it slurred, strange or hard to understand?
  • Time: if you see any of these signs, call for emergency help immediately. In Lagos, call 112 or 767.

Other warning signs

A stroke can also cause:

  • sudden numbness or weakness in a leg, often on one side of the body
  • sudden confusion or trouble understanding
  • sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes
  • sudden dizziness, loss of balance or difficulty walking
  • a sudden, severe headache unlike any before

The key word is sudden. Even if the signs seem to pass after a few minutes, treat it as an emergency. A "mini-stroke" is often a warning that a larger one may follow.

What to do while you wait

  1. Note the time the symptoms started. Doctors will need this to decide on treatment.
  2. Keep the person comfortable and safe. If they are drowsy, lay them on their side.
  3. Don't give food, drink or medicines, including aspirin. Swallowing may be affected, and some strokes are caused by bleeding.
  4. Don't wait to see if things improve, and don't take them to a pastor, an herbalist or a chemist first.

Why time matters

Some strokes can be treated with medicines that dissolve the clot, but these work only within the first few hours. A hospital will usually need a brain scan first to see what kind of stroke it is.

Plan ahead, especially in Lagos

Traffic and hospital capacity in Lagos mean planning ahead really matters.

  • Find out which hospitals near your family can do a CT scan 24 hours a day and have a stroke or emergency team.
  • Save their address and phone number in every family member's phone, and in your parent's phone.
  • Agree who will drive or call an ambulance, and who will meet them at the hospital.
  • If you live abroad, make sure someone in Lagos has the plan too.

Preventing a stroke

High blood pressure is the biggest single risk factor for stroke. Regular checks, taking medicines as prescribed, not smoking, staying active and managing diabetes all lower the risk. See our guide to checking blood pressure at home.

In an emergency, call 112 or 767 in Lagos, or go to the nearest hospital now. Don't wait for a reply from anyone online.
