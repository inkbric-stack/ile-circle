How to check your blood pressure at home, properly

Home readings are only useful if they're taken the right way. A simple routine you can follow, or share with a parent in Lagos.

By Ilé Editorial Team21 October 20262 min read

High blood pressure rarely causes symptoms, which is why so many people don't know they have it. Checking at home gives you and your doctor a much clearer picture than a single reading at a clinic, where nerves alone can push the numbers up.

But a home reading is only useful if it's taken the right way. Here is a routine that works.

Choose the right monitor

  • Use an automatic monitor with an upper-arm cuff. Wrist and finger monitors are less reliable.
  • Look for a model that has been clinically validated. Your doctor or pharmacist can help you choose.
  • Make sure the cuff fits. A cuff that is too small gives readings that are too high. Large arms need a large cuff.

Before you measure

  • Avoid caffeine, cigarettes and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand.
  • Empty your bladder.
  • Sit quietly for 5 minutes in a chair with back support. No phone, no television, no conversation.

How to sit

  • Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed.
  • Rest your arm on a table so the cuff is at the level of your heart.
  • Put the cuff on bare skin, not over a sleeve, about two finger-widths above the bend of your elbow.

Taking the reading

  1. Press start and stay still and silent while the cuff inflates.
  2. Wait one minute, then take a second reading.
  3. Write down both readings, the date and the time. Many monitors store them, but a notebook or phone note you can show your doctor is just as good.

Measure at the same times each day, for example once in the morning before medicines and once in the evening. Your doctor may ask you to do this for a week before an appointment.

What the numbers mean

A reading has two numbers, such as 128/82. The first is the pressure when your heart beats; the second is the pressure between beats.

Your doctor will tell you what target is right for you. As a general guide, many international guidelines consider home readings that are regularly 135/85 or higher to be raised, and worth discussing with a doctor.

Get emergency help if a reading is very high, for example 180/120 or above, and you have chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness, difficulty speaking, changes in vision or a severe headache. Call 112 or 767 in Lagos, or go to the nearest hospital.

If you're helping a parent from abroad

  • Buy the monitor together and do the first reading on a video call.
  • Agree on a simple routine: two readings, morning and evening, sent to you or a family member each week.
  • Look at the pattern over a week, not a single number, and share it with their doctor.

Never change or stop blood pressure medicine because of home readings alone. Take your readings to your doctor and decide together.

This article gives general information, not medical advice. Always talk to the doctor who knows you before making changes to your treatment. In an emergency, call 112 or 767 in Lagos, or go to the nearest hospital.
