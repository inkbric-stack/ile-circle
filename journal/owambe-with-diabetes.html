Enjoying owambe season with diabetes

Parties are part of life. How to enjoy the jollof, the small chops and the dancing without your sugar levels spiking.

By Ilé Editorial Team15 October 20262 min read

Weddings, birthdays, naming ceremonies, funerals: in Nigeria, the party never really stops. Having diabetes doesn't mean you can't enjoy them. It means planning a little.

Before you go

  • Don't arrive starving. Have a small, balanced meal or snack before you leave, so you aren't tempted to overload your plate.
  • Take your medicines as usual, and carry them with you if the party runs late. If you use insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar, ask your doctor how to handle a late or large meal.
  • Carry water, and something sweet in case your sugar drops too low.

Building your plate

Use the plate method as a simple guide:

  • Half the plate: vegetables, salad, or vegetable soups such as efo riro.
  • A quarter: protein, such as grilled chicken, fish, turkey or moi moi.
  • A quarter: starch, such as one serving spoon of jollof or fried rice, or a small portion of swallow.

Choose grilled or peppered over deep-fried when you can, and go easy on fried rice, puff-puff and small chops.

Drinks

Soft drinks, malt and fruit juice can contain a lot of sugar in a single bottle. Choose water, sparkling water, unsweetened zobo, or zero-sugar drinks. If you drink alcohol, talk to your doctor first, never drink on an empty stomach, and keep to small amounts.

Cake and dessert

Have a small slice rather than none at all. Enjoy it slowly, ideally after your meal rather than on its own.

Keep moving

Dancing counts as exercise. A few songs on the dance floor help your body use the sugar from your meal.

The day after

Check your sugar the next morning if you monitor at home, drink plenty of water and get back to your usual routine. One party won't undo your progress. It's the everyday pattern that matters.

Everyone's diabetes is different. Talk to your doctor or dietitian about the targets and portions that are right for you.

This article gives general information, not medical advice. Always talk to the doctor who knows you before making changes to your treatment. In an emergency, call 112 or 767 in Lagos, or go to the nearest hospital.
