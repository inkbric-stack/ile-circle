Caring for a parent in Nigeria from abroad: a practical guide

Ten practical steps for children in London, Houston or Toronto who want their parents in Lagos to be safe, well cared for and independent.

By Ilé Editorial Team18 October 20262 min read

Millions of Nigerians abroad look after parents back home. Most of us do it in pieces: a transfer here, a call to a cousin there, a worried message at 2am. These steps can make it calmer, safer and fairer for everyone.

1. Have the conversation early

Talk to your parents about their health and wishes while things are going well. Ask what matters most to them: staying in their own home, being near church or mosque, keeping their independence. Listen more than you speak.

2. Build a one-page health summary

Keep a single page, shared with siblings, that lists:

  • their conditions and allergies
  • every medicine, the dose and when they take it
  • their doctors, hospital and HMO details
  • their blood group, if known

Update it after every appointment.

3. Know their doctor, and their nearest emergency hospital

Find out who their regular doctor is and how to reach them. Identify the nearest hospital with a 24-hour emergency department that can handle a heart attack or stroke. Save the address and numbers in your phone and theirs.

4. Make an emergency plan

Agree who on the ground can get to them within an hour, who has a spare key, and who pays the hospital deposit if needed. Write it down and share it.

5. Keep medicines flowing

Running out of medicine is one of the most common reasons conditions get worse. Use a trusted pharmacy, set up regular refills, and check that the medicines are genuine.

6. Make check-ins useful

A weekly call is more useful when it has a little structure. Ask about sleep, appetite, mood, any falls, and their blood pressure or sugar readings if they check them.

7. Make the home safe

Falls are a major risk for older people. Good lighting, rails in the bathroom, non-slip mats and a clear path to the toilet at night make a real difference.

8. Pay for care directly where you can

Money sent for medicines or a carer doesn't always reach them. Where possible, pay the pharmacy, hospital or care provider directly, and ask for receipts.

9. Respect their independence

Your parents are adults who have looked after themselves for decades. Offer choices rather than instructions, and make sure any help you arrange is help they actually want.

10. Look after yourself too

Caring from a distance brings guilt and worry. Share the load with siblings, talk to others in the same position, and remember you don't have to do it all alone.
